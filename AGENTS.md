# Project verification

This repository contains independent procedural Blender scenes. Keep changes to one building isolated from the other generators. Blender 5.2.0 LTS is installed as `blender` in the current development environment.

## Abeno Harukas

- Build the editable scene, three exterior review images (preview, plan, north), and a core-structure view: `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python build_abeno_harukas.py`.
- Build without rendering: append `-- --no-render`.
- Render selected views: append `-- --views preview plan north core` with the desired subset. The `core` view temporarily hides the facade (mass) collections without changing visibility in the saved full model.
- Review massing only: append `-- --blockout --views preview north`. Blockout builds no core and does not save or replace the detailed `.blend`.
- Verify the saved scene: `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python verify_abeno_harukas.py`.
- Check syntax and whitespace: `python3 -m py_compile build_abeno_harukas.py verify_abeno_harukas.py` and `git diff --check`.
- Outputs are local files under `out/`, which is Git-ignored. File existence does not depend on Git tracking.
- Ground must match the union of the modeled mass footprints, not a large studio plane or padded rectangle. The main perspective camera is at 1.7 m eye height, looking upward; the initial orbit viewport must reconstruct the same eye position. Preserve complete building framing while changing the lens or target.
- Scene coordinates are X east, Y north, Z up, in metres. The massing is three ground-standing volumes connected horizontally south to north (Low 65 m, Middle 180 m, High 300 m), each a different quadrilateral plan defined in `MASSES`; adjacent volumes share their full connecting edge. The long connecting edges are slanted in plan — they tilt along the north-south axis instead of running straight east-west: the low/middle edge drops toward the east while the middle/high edge climbs toward the east. The middle volume's side edges flare outward at about 5 degrees toward its low-side face; its east side extends outward (its SE corner is the composition's outermost point) while its west side approaches the high volume's west edge. Other plan tilts stay within 3 degrees where the widened shared edge does not determine them. Flat roofs. Do not restore the vertically stacked tier scheme, `TIER_PLAN`/`plan_width`, rectangular plans, or sloped roofs.
- The low/middle connecting edge widens with the middle volume's 5-degree flare; it has no fixed 88 m width limit. The low and middle volumes share the full widened edge, and the middle volume narrows toward and past the high volume. Overall depth stays 86 m.
- The curtain wall runs continuously past the belt truss bands (their open 3D lattices sit recessed behind the glass at `TRUSS_INSET`, reading through it) and splits only around the exposed staggered mid-office trusses (`glass_spans` over the exposed bands). Never rebuild a solid backing wall or a dense interior web grid inside the bands: each band is a hollow ring of perimeter chevron trusses on the four faces (upward chevrons with both feet on the lower chord, boundary posts), see-through like the reference photo — no interior columns. Adjacent faces share one post at each corner (no doubled corner posts), and the slab at each band's bottom level stays so the truss ring stands on a floor plate. Only the facade hanging-truss mast and the staggered mid-office trusses stand exposed. Chevron groups derive their count from each face's width at the fixed ~12 m `TRUSS_SPACING`, so counts may change as a face widens while the span stays fixed. Truss members are white-painted steel (`Harukas_Truss_White`, per the reference photos): chunky 1.8 m posts (`TRUSS_POST_W`), 0.7 m diagonals (`TRUSS_DIAG_W`).
- Truss band tops sit just below the low/middle rooflines at z 80/195 m plus one near the tower top at z 275-285 m (floors 56-57, just below the 58-60F observatory floors) (`TRUSS_BAND_TOPS`, each belt band 7.5 m tall — 1.5x the one-storey staggered trusses, per the figure's proportions); each belt band runs through every volume tall enough to carry it (`mass_bands` returns (z0, z1, exposed) triples). Between the roof bands sit two staggered mid-office trusses, each one storey tall (`TRUSS_BAND_H_PARTIAL` = 5 m) and wrapping a single volume only (`TRUSS_BAND_TOPS_PARTIAL`: floors 25-26 = z 125-130 on the middle volume, floors 31-32 = z 155-160 on the high volume) — they do not run through the adjoining volume and stand exposed (no glazing), per the published void-structure figure. No truss band at the ground level or at the 300 m roof. Reference figures saved in `docs/images/harukas_truss_void_structure_fig4.png` (floor labels 15F / 25,26F / 31,32F / 37F) and `docs/images/harukas_truss_floors_fig7.png` (floor axis with the 56-57F top band).
- A single vertical hanging-truss mast runs on the wider side face at each side of the composition — the side with three chevron groups: the middle volume's EAST face on the east side and the high volume's WEST face on the west side (`FACADE_MASTS`, `facade_mast`/`facade_masts`): one strip near the middle/high junction, from the low-roof belt band up to the middle-roof belt band (z 80-187.5 m), stopping at the exposed staggered band it meets (the band chords carry it across — no mast members inside the bands). The mast is a vertical extension of the main trusses: exactly one chevron bay wide, snapped to the face's chevron grid with the band trusses' own group count and `edge_point` inset, so its posts continue the belt bands' posts exactly. It uses chunky posts matching the band posts (`TRUSS_POST_W` = 1.8) with upward pyramid (A-frame) cells stacked vertically (`MAST_CELL_H`, two storeys per cell): each cell's two diagonals run from the bottom corners to the apex at the top centre — not zigzag, not stacked X braces. Like every truss, the masts stay within the envelope, recessed at `TRUSS_INSET` behind the curtain-wall glass — never let them protrude past the facade plane. No facade masts on the low volume or the tower; the tower-zone masts stay interior (the braced atrium flank walls).
- Harukas glazing reuses `materials.make_glass`: EEVEE uses the house's glossy/transparent preview, while a separate Cycles output uses the physical Principled shader. Keep roughness and glossy filtering at zero and transmission at one for all pane variants; use a near-white blue tint. Material Preview uses Blender's bundled `forest.exr`.
- Ordinary storey bays use separate upper main glass and lower short glass, divided by a thin transom. `SHORT_GLASS_FRACTION = 0.25` is a photo-estimated proportion; the backing belongs behind the lower quarter, not the upper vision pane. Truss recesses, transition slivers and observatory glazing are excluded. Render `-- --views facade` for the close-up; preserve the narrow vertical module and avoid oversized horizontal bands.
- The curtain-wall frame (`facade_grid`: vertical mullions at the `MULLION_SPACING` module plus the storey/split transoms) is white-painted aluminium (`Harukas_Facade_Frame`, the same white as the truss steel) and runs the full volume height on all four faces. It does not break where the glass splits around the exposed staggered trusses, so the long and short faces read identically with one continuous frame from bottom to top.
- Keep ordinary window vision areas free of full-height opaque backing walls. Harukas shadowboxes belong only behind recessed service/transfer zones; interior visibility is checked separately from glass shader properties with short inward and floor-directed rays.
- Interior roller blinds follow the Office reference (`make_frosted_glass_film`): one thin frosted panel per curtain-wall module behind each storey's vision glass, hung `CURTAIN_GAP` = 10 cm inside the glass line, for every storey that has a floor slab. Three discrete states — rolled up (no panel), half down, fully down — are distributed in exactly balanced counts from a fixed `CURTAIN_SEED` and shuffled, so all panes are covered but the mix is even. The one merged `Harukas_Curtains` object lives in the `Harukas_Curtains` collection, which the core review view hides with the facades.
- Ceiling strip lights follow the house reference but cover the whole ceiling, not just a perimeter band: `LIGHT_ROW_PITCH` = 3 m rows of long thin fixtures (`append_strip`, `LIGHT_STRIP_W`/`H`) run along the plan's long axis across every floor with a slab, so no area of the plate is left dark. Each row is clipped against the plan (`line_span_in_quad`) and around the modelled core openings (`subtract_intervals`), keeping `LIGHT_EDGE_CLEAR` = 0.6 m off the facade and the core, so the lights wrap the core while still covering the far side of the plate. A fixed `LIGHT_SEED` gives each strip a stable state — off, or lit daylight/warm (`LIGHT_ON_RATIO`) — merged into `Harukas_Ceiling_Lights_{Daylight,Warm,Off}` in the `Harukas_Lights` collection, which the core review view hides with the facades.
- Following the user's close-up correction, truss faces use complete upward chevrons with both ends on the lower chord and no terminal half-bay. Chevron count may change as a face widens, but the approximately 12 m span remains fixed; long N/S and short E/W faces both derive their group count from face width. Render the north 25F camera with `-- --views truss_north`; do not claim whole-building as-built accuracy from passing tests.
- Truss boundary posts and apex continuation columns are different components. Each long-face chevron apex has its own thicker `Perimeter_Apex_*` column extending upward; do not substitute glass mullions or only the eight in-band boundary posts. Terminate continuations at the next modeled transfer band, setback or observatory base, keeping the full column section inside the envelope. `-- --views apex_columns` renders a landscape connection review.
- Core reconstruction follows the published linked-void scheme (`build_core` in the `Harukas_Core` collection): four lower corner service zones (z 0-80 m, every volume, on the 5 m inset plan corners), a braced steel middle spine with a west eco-void frame and east lift banks (z 80-195 m, middle and high volumes), and an upper atrium frame whose east/west flank walls are X-braced in two-storey cells up to the top truss band (z 195-262.5 m; crown floors above stay unbraced) plus a slim 8x12 m service core continuing to the roof (z 195-300 m, high volume only); the upper core stands just east of the atrium, stacking over the lift banks below. Horizontal transfer ties link corner zones to the spine at z 80 m and the spine to the atrium columns at z 195 m. Floor slabs (`core_floor`/`core_openings` in `Harukas_Floors`, holed 2D curves) run every 5 m with actual openings where the core passes, inset 0.3 m from the plan, tops flush with the storey level, staying clear of the open truss bands; the slab at each band's bottom level stays so the truss ring stands on a floor plate. Slab edges stop 5 cm short of core walls. Test clearances, opening corners and transfer-tie endpoints when changing these systems. Core dimensions and internal equipment are schematic, not as-built.
- Use integer indices in generated object names instead of raw floating-point elevations. In this local Blender setup, a name ending in `.94444444444446` triggered a fatal `std::out_of_range: stoi`; indexed names allow construction and reload to complete.
- The generator stores reference URLs and approximation limits in scene custom properties. Geometry checks validate the chosen model constraints, not exact correspondence to the real building; also inspect multiple rendered views.
