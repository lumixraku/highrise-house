# Project verification

This repository contains independent procedural Blender scenes. Keep changes to one building isolated from the other generators. Blender 5.2.0 LTS is installed as `blender` in the current development environment.

## Abeno Harukas

- Build the editable scene, three exterior review images (preview, plan, north), and a core-structure view: `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python build_abeno_harukas.py`.
- Build without rendering: append `-- --no-render`.
- Render selected views: append `-- --views preview plan north core` with the desired subset. The `core` view temporarily hides the facade (mass) collections without changing visibility in the saved full model.
- Review massing only: append `-- --blockout --views preview north`. Blockout builds no core and does not save or replace the detailed `.blend`.
- Verify the saved scene: `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python verify_abeno_harukas.py`.
- Check syntax and whitespace: `python3 -m py_compile build_abeno_harukas.py verify_abeno_harukas.py` and `git diff --check`.
- Outputs are local files under `out/`, which is Git-ignored. File existence does not depend on Git tracking.
- Ground must match the union of the modeled mass footprints, not a large studio plane or padded rectangle. The main perspective camera is at 1.7 m eye height, looking upward; the initial orbit viewport must reconstruct the same eye position. Preserve complete building framing while changing the lens or target.
- Scene coordinates are X east, Y north, Z up, in metres. The massing is three ground-standing volumes connected horizontally south to north (Low 80 m, Middle 195 m, High 300 m), each a different quadrilateral plan defined in `MASSES`; adjacent volumes share their full connecting edge. The long connecting edges are slanted in plan — they tilt along the north-south axis instead of running straight east-west: the low/middle edge drops toward the east while the middle/high edge climbs toward the east. Every plan tilt stays within 3 degrees of its dominant axis, as in the user's sketch. The middle volume's east side extends outward (its SE corner is the composition's outermost point) while its west side approaches the high volume's west edge. Flat roofs. Do not restore the vertically stacked tier scheme, `TIER_PLAN`/`plan_width`, rectangular plans, or sloped roofs.
- The low/middle connecting edge (88 m) is the composition's widest line: the low and middle volumes both flare outward toward it, so the middle volume is widest at the face joining the lowest volume (side edges lean out at about 3 degrees, within the general cap). It narrows toward and past the high volume. Overall depth stays 86 m.
- The curtain wall runs continuously past the belt truss bands (their open 3D lattices sit recessed behind the glass at `TRUSS_INSET`, reading through it) and splits only around the exposed staggered mid-office trusses (`glass_spans` over the exposed bands). Never rebuild a solid backing wall or a dense interior web grid inside the bands: each band is a hollow ring of perimeter chevron trusses on the four faces (upward chevrons with both feet on the lower chord, boundary posts), see-through like the reference photo — no interior columns. Adjacent faces share one post at each corner (no doubled corner posts), and the slab at each band's bottom level stays so the truss ring stands on a floor plate. Only the facade hanging-truss mast and the staggered mid-office trusses stand exposed. Chevron groups follow a ~12 m spacing (`TRUSS_SPACING`) so density matches the seven-group long faces; short side faces get 2-3 groups. Truss members are white-painted steel (`Harukas_Truss_White`, per the reference photos): chunky 1.8 m posts (`TRUSS_POST_W`), 0.7 m diagonals (`TRUSS_DIAG_W`).
- Truss band tops sit just below the low/middle rooflines at z 80/195 m plus one near the tower top at z 275-285 m (floors 56-57, just below the 58-60F observatory floors) (`TRUSS_BAND_TOPS`, each belt band 7.5 m tall — 1.5x the one-storey staggered trusses, per the figure's proportions); each belt band runs through every volume tall enough to carry it (`mass_bands` returns (z0, z1, exposed) triples). Between the roof bands sit two staggered mid-office trusses, each one storey tall (`TRUSS_BAND_H_PARTIAL` = 5 m) and wrapping a single volume only (`TRUSS_BAND_TOPS_PARTIAL`: floors 25-26 = z 125-130 on the middle volume, floors 31-32 = z 155-160 on the high volume) — they do not run through the adjoining volume and stand exposed (no glazing), per the published void-structure figure. No truss band at the ground level or at the 300 m roof. Reference figures saved in `docs/images/harukas_truss_void_structure_fig4.png` (floor labels 15F / 25,26F / 31,32F / 37F) and `docs/images/harukas_truss_floors_fig7.png` (floor axis with the 56-57F top band).
- A single vertical hanging-truss mast runs on each of the middle volume's east and west facades (`FACADE_MASTS`, `facade_mast`/`facade_masts`): one strip near the middle/high junction (y ≈ +11), from the 15F belt band up to the 37F belt band (z 80-187.5 m), stopping at the exposed staggered band it meets (the band chords carry it across — no mast members inside the bands). The mast is a vertical extension of the main trusses: exactly one chevron bay wide, snapped to the face's chevron grid so its posts continue the belt bands' posts. It uses chunky posts matching the band posts (`TRUSS_POST_W` = 1.8) with upward pyramid (A-frame) cells stacked vertically (`MAST_CELL_H`, two storeys per cell): each cell's two diagonals run from the bottom corners to the apex at the top centre — not zigzag, not stacked X braces. Like every truss, the masts stay within the envelope, recessed at `TRUSS_INSET` behind the curtain-wall glass — never let them protrude past the facade plane. No facade masts on the low volume or the tower; the tower-zone masts stay interior (the braced atrium flank walls).
- Harukas glazing reuses `materials.make_glass`: EEVEE uses the house's glossy/transparent preview, while a separate Cycles output uses the physical Principled shader. Keep roughness and glossy filtering at zero and transmission at one for all pane variants; use a near-white blue tint. Material Preview uses Blender's bundled `forest.exr`.
- Ordinary storey bays use separate upper main glass and lower short glass, divided by a thin transom. `SHORT_GLASS_FRACTION = 0.25` is a photo-estimated proportion; the backing belongs behind the lower quarter, not the upper vision pane. Truss recesses, transition slivers and observatory glazing are excluded. Render `-- --views facade` for the close-up; preserve the narrow vertical module and avoid oversized horizontal bands.
- Keep ordinary window vision areas free of full-height opaque backing walls. Harukas shadowboxes belong only behind recessed service/transfer zones; interior visibility is checked separately from glass shader properties with short inward and floor-directed rays.
- Following the user's close-up correction, long N/S truss faces use seven complete upward chevrons (14 diagonals, eight boundary posts), with both ends on the lower chord and no terminal half-bay. North 25F is independently photo-counted; the other long faces apply the requested reference pattern, not independently surveyed counts. Short E/W counts remain provisional and must not inherit seven bays automatically. Render the north 25F camera with `-- --views truss_north`; do not claim whole-building as-built accuracy from passing tests.
- Truss boundary posts and apex continuation columns are different components. Each long-face chevron apex has its own thicker `Perimeter_Apex_*` column extending upward; do not substitute glass mullions or only the eight in-band boundary posts. Terminate continuations at the next modeled transfer band, setback or observatory base, keeping the full column section inside the envelope. `-- --views apex_columns` renders a landscape connection review.
- Core reconstruction follows the published linked-void scheme (`build_core` in the `Harukas_Core` collection): four lower corner service zones (z 0-80 m, every volume, on the 5 m inset plan corners), a braced steel middle spine with a west eco-void frame and east lift banks (z 80-195 m, middle and high volumes), and an upper atrium frame whose east/west flank walls are X-braced in two-storey cells up to the top truss band (z 195-277.5 m; crown floors above stay unbraced) plus a slim 8x12 m service core continuing to the roof (z 195-300 m, high volume only); the upper core stands just east of the atrium, stacking over the lift banks below. Horizontal transfer ties link corner zones to the spine at z 80 m and the spine to the atrium columns at z 195 m. Floor slabs (`core_floor`/`core_openings` in `Harukas_Floors`, holed 2D curves) run every 5 m with actual openings where the core passes, inset 0.3 m from the plan, tops flush with the storey level, staying clear of the open truss bands; the slab at each band's bottom level stays so the truss ring stands on a floor plate. Slab edges stop 5 cm short of core walls. Test clearances, opening corners and transfer-tie endpoints when changing these systems. Core dimensions and internal equipment are schematic, not as-built.
- Use integer indices in generated object names instead of raw floating-point elevations. In this local Blender setup, a name ending in `.94444444444446` triggered a fatal `std::out_of_range: stoi`; indexed names allow construction and reload to complete.
- The generator stores reference URLs and approximation limits in scene custom properties. Geometry checks validate the chosen model constraints, not exact correspondence to the real building; also inspect multiple rendered views.
- The repository currently uses root `progress.md` for completed changes and verification results; `docs/progress.md` does not exist.
